# 5. Research Roadmap and Safeguards

## 5.1 Research questions

1. Which properties of living systems can be translated into testable design principles?
2. How can local and embodied knowledge be represented without stripping away context?
3. What measures capture resilience, care, repair, and ecological balance?
4. When does a nature analogy clarify a design problem, and when does it mislead us?
5. How can AI remain useful without becoming the final authority?
6. What forms of governance allow communities to correct, refuse, or redesign a system?

## 5.2 Proposed phases

### Phase 1 — Vocabulary and evidence

Define terms, separate established findings from hypotheses, collect references, and invite criticism from multiple disciplines.

### Phase 2 — Small observations

Document real examples of adaptation, cooperation, repair, and self-organization in organisms, communities, and institutions.

### Phase 3 — Low-risk prototypes

Build small tools or practices with clear boundaries, human review, data minimization, and rollback plans.

### Phase 4 — Comparative evaluation

Compare OI-inspired approaches with conventional approaches across performance, energy, resilience, equity, and user trust.

### Phase 5 — Responsible scale

Only scale systems that have transparent governance, independent review, maintenance ownership, failure recovery, and a way for affected people to appeal.

## 5.3 Evidence discipline

Every document should distinguish:

- **Observation:** what was seen or measured;
- **Interpretation:** what the observation may mean;
- **Proposal:** what we suggest trying;
- **Uncertainty:** what is not yet known;
- **Decision:** what action is justified now.

## 5.4 Ethical and ecological safeguards

- Do not use “organic” to imply safety, purity, or moral superiority.
- Do not claim consciousness, empathy, or moral agency for a system without evidence.
- Obtain consent when collecting lived experience or sensitive biological data.
- Avoid extracting local or Indigenous knowledge without recognition and benefit-sharing.
- Report negative results and unintended effects.
- Consider energy, water, materials, labour, and disposal across the full lifecycle.
- Keep humans accountable for decisions that affect rights, health, livelihood, or safety.
